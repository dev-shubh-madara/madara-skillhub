# Business frameworks

## One-line idea

"For <customer> who <problem>, <name> is a <type of product> that <main benefit>. We make money by <model>."

## Unit economics (show the working)

```
Price per sale (P)               = 499
Direct cost per sale (C)         = 120   (materials, payment fees, delivery)
Contribution per sale (P - C)    = 379
Fixed costs per month (F)        = 6000  (hosting, tools, rent)
Break-even sales per month       = F / (P - C) = 6000 / 379 = 16 (round up)
```

For subscriptions also estimate: monthly churn, customer lifetime = 1 / churn, lifetime value = monthly contribution x lifetime, and compare it with the cost to get one customer.

Mark every number that came from you, not the user, as an assumption.

## Testing demand in a week

| Method | Signal that counts |
|---|---|
| 10 to 15 customer conversations | They describe the problem without being led, and already spend time or money on it |
| Landing page with a sign-up | Real sign-ups from people who are not friends |
| Pre-order or deposit | Money paid, even small |
| Manual version | Someone uses it twice |

Good interview questions ask about the past: "When did this last happen? What did you do? What did it cost you?" Avoid "Would you buy this?" since people answer politely. The book The Mom Test by Rob Fitzpatrick covers this well.

## Pricing approaches

- Cost plus: cost times a margin. Simple, often too low.
- Competitor based: place yourself against two or three named alternatives.
- Value based: a share of the money or time you save the customer.
Start with value, check against competitors, make sure it is above cost plus.

## First customers

Write down where they already gather (college groups, local shops, online communities, LinkedIn), then a short first message: who you are, the problem you noticed, a specific offer, an easy yes.

## Legal and money flags

Before taking payments, check with a local accountant or official government site: business registration, tax registration and its current threshold, invoicing rules, payment gateway requirements, and any licence the activity needs. Rules differ by country and change, so do not state a threshold from memory.
