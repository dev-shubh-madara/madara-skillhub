# Career builder

## Before you start

Ask for the target role or field, the year of study or years of experience, and the real projects, skills and results they have. Use only what they give you.

## Workflow

1. **Set the target.** One role, one kind of company. Everything else follows from it.
2. **Inventory.** List projects, tools, courses and any measurable results (users, speed, money saved, grades).
3. **Write the resume.**
   - One page for students and early career.
   - Each project: what it is, what the user built, the stack, and a result if one exists.
   - Start bullets with action verbs, keep them specific, cut filler words.
4. **LinkedIn.** Headline with role and focus, an About section in plain first-person voice, featured projects with links, and a skills list matching the target.
5. **Portfolio plan.** Pick two or three projects that prove the target role. For each, write what to polish: README, demo link, screenshots, a short write-up.
6. **Interview prep.** Likely questions for the role, with the user's own projects as answers. Practice one at a time and give feedback.
7. **Plan.** A month-by-month list of what to build, learn and apply to.

## Quality bar

- Every claim on the resume can be backed up in an interview.
- Numbers come from the user, never invented.
- The text sounds like the person, not like a template.
- ATS-friendly: plain headings, no tables or graphics in the main resume.

## Avoid

- Adding skills or experience the user does not have.
- Buzzword lists with nothing behind them.
- Copying someone else's profile word for word. Use it as a model for structure only.
- Promising offers or timelines.
