# Source guide and templates

## Source tiers

1. Primary: peer-reviewed papers, official statistics, standards, laws, company filings, official documentation.
2. Strong secondary: review articles, textbooks, reputable news with named sources.
3. Leads only: blogs, forums, social posts, AI summaries. Use them to find tier 1 or 2 sources, never cite them as proof.

Check the date. For anything that changes (prices, versions, laws, who holds a role), use the newest source and state the date.

## Claim check table (keep while writing)

| Claim | Source | Date | Second source agrees? | Used where |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Any important claim with "no" in the fourth column is written as uncertain or removed.

## Citation styles

APA 7
```
Author, A. A., & Author, B. B. (Year). Title of the article. Journal Name, volume(issue), pages. https://doi.org/xxxx
Organisation Name. (Year, Month Day). Title of the page. Site Name. URL
```

IEEE (common in engineering)
```
[1] A. Author and B. Author, "Title of the article," Journal Abbrev., vol. x, no. x, pp. xx-xx, Month Year, doi: xxxx.
[2] Organisation, "Title of the page." Site. URL (accessed Mon. Day, Year).
```

Use whichever the user's course asks for. Never fill a citation field you could not confirm.

## Report template

```
Title
Summary (the answer in 3 to 5 sentences)
1. Background (only what the reader needs)
2. One section per research question
3. Comparison table if options are compared
4. What is uncertain or disputed
5. Conclusion and recommendation
References
Not verified (claims you could not confirm)
```

## Writing in your own words

Read the passage, close it, write the point from memory, then check you kept the meaning and did not keep the sentence shape. Quote only when the exact words matter, keep quotes short, and cite them.
