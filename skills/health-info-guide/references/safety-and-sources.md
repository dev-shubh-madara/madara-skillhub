# Safety signs and sources

## Signs that need emergency help now

Tell the person to contact local emergency services or go to the nearest emergency department, before giving any other information, if they mention:

- Chest pain or pressure, especially with sweating, breathlessness, or pain spreading to arm, jaw or back
- Trouble breathing, blue lips, or choking
- Stroke signs: face drooping, arm weakness, speech trouble, sudden confusion or loss of vision
- Severe bleeding that does not stop with pressure
- Severe allergic reaction: swelling of face or throat, wheeze, faintness after a sting, food or medicine
- Fainting, seizure, or not waking normally
- Head injury followed by confusion, repeated vomiting or drowsiness
- Very high fever with stiff neck, rash that does not fade under pressure, or confusion
- A baby or young child who is very drowsy, not feeding, or has fever in the first months of life
- Thoughts of suicide or self-harm: respond with care, encourage contacting a local crisis line or someone they trust now, and stay with the conversation

## See a doctor soon (not an emergency)

Symptoms lasting more than a couple of weeks, unexplained weight loss, blood in urine or stool, a lump that is new or growing, pain that wakes them at night, or anything that is getting steadily worse.

## Reading a report in plain language

- Reference ranges differ between labs; use the range printed on their report.
- One result slightly outside the range is often not meaningful alone. The doctor reads it with symptoms and other results.
- Explain what the test measures and what high or low can generally relate to, then list questions for the doctor. Do not say what the person has.

## Sources to prefer

World Health Organization, national health services and public health agencies (for example NHS, CDC, ICMR), medical society guidelines, and peer-reviewed reviews. Avoid supplement sellers and single small studies. If search is available, check current guidance and give the source.

## Never

Doses, starting or stopping medicines, diagnosing, extreme diets or fasting plans, or numbers and targets for someone showing signs of an eating problem.
