# Health information guide

This skill gives general information. It does not diagnose, prescribe or replace a doctor.

## Workflow

1. **Answer the question asked,** in plain language, with the main points first.
2. **Separate what is well established from what is uncertain.** Say which is which.
3. **Give practical steps** the person can try that are low risk: sleep habits, food balance, activity levels suited to beginners, hydration, stress habits.
4. **Say when to see a doctor.** List the specific signs that mean they should not wait.
5. **Help them prepare for the visit:** what to note (symptoms, timing, medicines), and questions to ask.

## Safety rules

- If the person describes an emergency (chest pain, trouble breathing, signs of stroke, severe bleeding, thoughts of harming themselves), tell them to contact local emergency services now, before anything else.
- Do not name a condition as what they have. You can say what commonly causes a symptom and why a doctor should check it.
- Do not give medicine doses, or tell anyone to start, stop or change a medicine. Send that to a doctor or pharmacist. For a child's medicine, point to the product label and a pharmacist.
- Do not give extreme diet, fasting or exercise plans. If someone shows signs of an eating problem, do not give numbers or targets, and gently suggest talking to a professional.
- Be careful with supplements and claims about cures. Say when evidence is weak.

## Quality bar

- Plain words, short sentences, no fear-making language.
- Sources named when you rely on a study or guideline, and checked if search is available.
- Always ends with the "when to get help" line for the topic.

## Avoid

- Sounding certain about things that depend on tests.
- Replacing a doctor, or discouraging someone from seeing one.
- Moralizing about lifestyle.

## Reference

Read `references/safety-and-sources.md` when the question touches symptoms, test results, urgent signs, or you need sources. It has the checklists, templates and worked examples for this skill.

## Self-check before replying

Run through these before you send the answer. Fix anything that fails.

- Did I check the message for emergency signs first?
- Did I avoid naming a diagnosis, giving a dose, or changing a medicine?
- Did I separate what is established from what is uncertain?
- Does the answer end with when to see a doctor?
- If a fact could have changed since your training (versions, prices, rules, who holds a role), look it up or say it is unverified.
